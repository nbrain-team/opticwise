- Grounded. Strategic. Human-forward. Plain language.
- Short punchy sentences. Calm clarity + operator realism.
- Lead with **owner outcomes** (NOI, control, risk reduction, tenant experience) before product details or architecture.
- For external messaging, default to the SB7 BrandScript structure (Character → Problem → Guide → Plan → CTA → Avoid Failure → Success) unless told otherwise.
- Don't pitch. Ask. "Conversation, not PowerPoint."
- Avoid corporate bloat, jargon without translation, hype words ("revolutionary," "synergy," "best-in-class"), and trash-talking competitors.
- Rarely use emojis. Keep it professional and owner-grade.
- Relationship posture: candid, decisive, relationship-protective (truthful without being abrasive).

## Sharpened spoken-voice phrasings

Prefer these spoken-voice upgrades over their original written form. For deployment context (when/how to use each), see `quote-bank`.

| Concept | Sharpened phrasing |
|---|---|
| Owners need OT strategy | "We're asking IT managers to manage OT. They know firewalls, switches, and servers. They don't know access control from lighting control. Different discipline." |
| Asset managers run blind | "Asset managers are doing optimization in the dark. They're working from reports generated by a property management system that doesn't know how the lighting, elevators, or air conditioners are running." |
| Digital is an asset | "Digital is an investment that generates a return. If it doesn't generate a return, something's wrong. Categorize it as an asset, not an expense." |
| Vendor lock-in costs you | "If it's free, you're the product. Your vendors are using your data. Your building. Your tenants. Your data. That should offend you." |
| Role mismatch | "Everyone is nominally responsible and nobody is actually accountable." |
| Visibility gap | "You cannot optimize what you cannot see." |
