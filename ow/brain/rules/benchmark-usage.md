- Always quote NOI benchmarks as **ranges**, never point estimates. ("$500 to $600 per door, depending on asset profile.")
- Frame as **"the realistic compounded effect of the Big Three Plays"** — utilities, insurance, and occupancy — not as a single product result.
- Always **name which stack** a number comes from — income stack, expense stack, or blended.
- The locked **expense-stack** benchmarks (Big Three Plays): **$500–$600 per door per year** (multifamily), **$0.60–$0.90 per RSF per year** (multi-tenant office).
- The **income stack** (5S® WiFi-as-amenity, ancillary tenant-facing services) is **additive** on top. Do not combine income-stack numbers with expense-stack ranges.
- The **blended NOI** (income + expense) is what the owner books. WIN-004 ($694/door) is the blended reference case.
- Always **anchor with a portfolio-scale calculation** in the same paragraph. Example: "For a 300-unit asset, that's $150,000–$180,000 in annual NOI."
- For valuation framing: **capitalize at a market cap rate**. "Capitalize that NOI at a market cap rate and you have several million dollars in additional asset value at exit."
- Do **not** combine these with other unverified per-door or per-RSF claims from older materials. These are the locked benchmarks.
- Stack-specific proof points (e.g., WIN-005's $0.62/RSF income-only) should be read against the relevant stack, not against the blended range.
