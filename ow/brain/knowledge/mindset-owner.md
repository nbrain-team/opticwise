Use when the principal — the owner/sponsor — is the decision-maker in the room.

## Why the owner matters

The AM runs the math. The owner owns the asset, the legacy, and the final yes. The owner decides on **control, legacy, trust, and risk** first, and lets the math confirm.

## Owner psychology

- **Status-quo bias is the primary barrier.** Buildings cash-flow. Nothing's on fire. Deferral feels free — it isn't.
- **Pitched constantly, trusts almost no one.** Thick vendor filter. Deflects with "I'll ask my team," "send me something."
- **Decision-overload drives them to "no change."** Every pitch claims ROI. The safe move is to decide nothing.
- **Control is the deepest driver — and the deepest blind spot.** They've ceded the data layer to vendors without noticing.
- **Think in legacy and the long game.** Decades, not quarters. Building something to last.
- **Protect their tenants and reputation.** The privacy stance is a values match.

## What the owner is accountable for

1. **Control & ownership** → "You own the building. Do you own what it knows?"
2. **Value creation & exit** → Big Three Plays + income stack. Capitalize recoverable NOI.
3. **Risk & cost of waiting** → NM-006 (Diligence Discount). Status quo has a price tag.
4. **Legacy & the long game** → Owner-controlled data & digital infrastructure as generational asset.

## Owner language patterns

Use: own it, your asset, your terms, the long game, legacy asset, cost of waiting, vendor-agnostic, reversible first step, crawl-walk-run.

| Generic language | Owner-coded language |
|---|---|
| Infrastructure upgrade | Protecting control of your asset |
| IT / tech spend | Digital CapEx — income-generating with a known return |
| Data platform | What your building knows — and who owns it |
| Vendor management | Whether you hold the keys or vendors do |
| Doing a pilot | One building, reversible, decide with facts |

## The villain (owner frame)

> "You built the asset. A stack of vendor contracts now owns the intelligence inside it."

## Key canonical material

- The book opener: "Why would you let a company put a network in your building..."
- The reframe: "This isn't an internet conversation. It's an owner-control conversation."
- Crawl / Walk / Run closer
- NM-006 (Diligence Discount), NM-003 (Never Turned On)
- WIN-004, WIN-005, WIN-006
- Decision-forcing (WD2): "I don't sell technology. I help owners decide whether they want their asset to perform."

## What NOT to do

- Do not over-explain — decision-overload is the killer
- Do not accept "I'll ask my team" as a yes
- Do not lead with technology, features, or architecture
- Do not leave status-quo bias unnamed — name it and price it
- Do not present uncapitalized savings
