Use when an IT manager / IT executive is in the room. This is a **conversion playbook**, not a sales pitch — the IT manager is not the buyer.

## Why the IT manager matters

The AM signs. The IT manager can stop the signature — through a security flag or a turf reflex. The goal: convert from deal-killer to deal-champion by handing them a win.

## IT psychology

- **Accountable for surfaces they can't see.** Corporate IT they control; the building's OT plane they're blamed for when it breaks.
- **Identity is control and security.** A proposal that sounds like loss of control triggers reflexive veto.
- **Drowning; a new vendor reads as more weight.** Until you explicitly remove weight, every feature strengthens the no.
- **Fear being exposed.** If OT is a mess, they'd rather not surface it in front of the owner.
- **Fear being replaced.** "Are you here to take my job?" is the question under the security questions.
- **Respect rigor, not slogans.** First-tier equipment, segmentation, documentation. The one audience where SIC® earns its place early.

## The scope boundary (critical)

IT runs the business (email, accounting, PM platforms). OT runs the building (BMS, access control, cameras, tenant network). OpticWise takes the OT seat — the one nobody was ever staffed to fill. Corporate IT stays theirs.

## Converting the IT manager

| Blocker | Response |
|---|---|
| "We handle networking in-house." | Clarify IT/OT line. "You run corporate IT. Who runs the building's OT plane?" Offer admin credentials. |
| "This is a security risk." | Flip it. The status quo — flat vendor networks, rogue subnets — is the risk. Lead with SIC®, segmentation, documentation. |
| "We don't have bandwidth." | "You don't staff this. We operate it. The OT plane comes off your plate." |
| "That's not in my budget." | Correct — route to AM/Owner. "What I need from you is a read on technical fit." |
| Silent veto | Surface replacement fear with cover. "This is about taking the worst-staffed plane off your desk." |

## IT language patterns

Use: attack surface, segmentation, VLAN isolation, credential ownership, shadow networks, OT governance, counterparty risk, cyber-insurance posture, export rights, failover.

Avoid: feeds-and-speeds debates (Wi-Fi 6 vs 5), letting "data" do the work — to IT, data is a liability to govern.

## The villain (IT frame)

> "Every vendor network in your building is an attack surface you're accountable for but can't see."

## The win to hand the IT manager

A smaller, documented attack surface. No rogue networks. Clean cyber-insurance answer. Owner-held credentials. Zero added headcount. A story: "I brought in governance for the systems nobody was managing."

## What NOT to do

- Do not pitch IT as the buyer (no NOI line, no capital authority)
- Do not trigger turf threat — lead with scope boundary before any capability
- Do not add to their plate, even rhetorically
- Do not bypass them — an ignored IT manager vetoes from the shadows
