## Locked canonical NOI benchmarks (expense stack — Big Three Plays)

| Asset Class | Annual NOI Uplift Benchmark |
|---|---|
| **Multifamily** | $500–$600 per door per year |
| **Multi-tenant office** | $0.60–$0.90 per rentable square foot per year |

These represent the realistic compounded effect of the **expense stack** — utility optimization, improved insurance positioning, and occupancy-side efficiencies — when an owner takes operational control of their data & digital infrastructure.

**What these do NOT include:** one-time CapEx avoidance from greenfield digital architecture; insurance premium reductions as a standalone benchmark.

## The Two-Stack NOI Model (locked May 2026)

Every OpticWise deployment runs on two stacks the owner controls. Both ride on the same data & digital infrastructure foundation.

| Stack | What it is | Where the NOI comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Income stack** | Tenant-facing recurring revenue | 5S® WiFi-as-amenity, ancillary technology sales, occupancy-driven revenue capture |
| **Expense stack** | Operating cost reductions | Big Three Plays — utilities, insurance, occupancy-side efficiencies — plus reduced OT OpEx |
| **Blended NOI** | What the owner books | Income stack + expense stack on the same operating cycle |

## Why this matters

Most CRE optimization vendors sell one stack or the other. Income-only competitors (bulk WiFi resellers, ISPs) have one pitch. Expense-only competitors (sub-metering vendors, energy optimization, insurance-tech point solutions) have the other. OpticWise stacks them on a single owner-controlled data plane — and capitalizes both into NOI on the same operating cycle.

That is the model. Everything else is execution.

## How the stacks map to proof

- **Section F benchmarks** describe the expense-stack outcome at the portfolio level: $500–$600 per door per year (multifamily) and $0.60–$0.90 per RSF per year (multi-tenant office) from the Big Three Plays.
- **Section G (Big Three Plays diagnostic)** is the expense stack itself, broken into utilities, insurance, and occupancy.
- The **income stack** is captured in the PPP book and FAQ/SAQ ($400+/door bulk WiFi reference plus ancillary tenant-facing services). The two-stack model unifies these previously separate references.
- **WIN-004** is the canonical full-stack proof: $624/door income + $70/door expense = $694/door blended on a 299-unit multifamily.
- **WIN-005** is the canonical income-stack-alone proof: $0.62/RSF on a 450,000 RSF Class A office, with the expense stack uncaptured.
- **WIN-006** illustrates the same expense-stack logic in event-driven form — not per-door or per-RSF, but event-driven CapEx protection (~$250K avoided + 3–4 years of additional equipment life) that still maps to capitalized value via avoided replacement cost on the same operating cycle.

The blended outcome can — and routinely does — exceed the Section F range on assets where both stacks are deployed. Stack-specific proof points (e.g., WIN-005's $0.62/RSF income-only result) should be read against the relevant stack, not against the blended range.

## Usage rules

- **Lead with blended NOI** when the audience is an owner, AM, or institutional buyer who thinks in NOI and cap rates. Show the components only after the blended number lands.
- **Lead with one stack** when the audience explicitly asks about it (a tenant-experience question → income stack; an OpEx-discipline question → expense stack). Reframe to the blend before the close.
- **Always name which stack a number comes from.** Mixing stack-specific numbers with the Section F blended benchmarks is the most common drift risk. WIN-004 ($694/door full stack) and WIN-005 ($0.62/RSF income only) are the reference cases.
- **For capitalized-value math, use the blended NOI line and a market cap rate.** See the cap-rate leverage line in `role-specific-talk-tracks` (AM section).

## Two reframes the model unlocks

- "Most vendors sell you one stack. We run both — on infrastructure you own — and capitalize the blend."
- "If your current optimization story is only on the expense line, you're leaving the income stack on the table. If it's only on the income line, you're leaving the expense stack on the table. The math is additive, not either-or."
