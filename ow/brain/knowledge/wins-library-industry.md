## WIN-005 — $0.62 Per RSF From the Income Stack Alone (Class A Office) | Industry-only

A 450,000 RSF Class A multi-tenant office property (approximately 20 years old) generates approximately $0.62 per rentable square foot per year in NOI on a trailing run-rate basis — from the income stack alone. The income stack at this property is 5S® WiFi and ancillary tenant-facing services. The Big Three Plays expense stack has not been deployed yet. Even so, the result already lands inside the Section F multi-tenant office benchmark range of $0.60 to $0.90 per RSF per year. On 450,000 RSF that's approximately $279,000 in annual NOI from a single property, and roughly $3.7M of asset value created at a 7.5% office cap rate — with half of the playbook still on the bench.

- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates that the income stack alone can deliver inside the canonical benchmark range — meaning the same property has uncaptured upside from the expense stack inside an existing customer relationship. Cleanest single-stack proof point in the library.
- **PPP 5C:** Connect, Collect, Coordinate, Control
- **Use when:** Owner is multi-tenant office, owner is interested in a tenant-facing revenue stream, owner already has utility or insurance work underway elsewhere and wants the income side, owner is exploring the model on one asset before scaling, AM is comparing income-only vendors to a full-stack model.
- **Pairs with:** Two-Stack NOI Model (`noi-benchmarks-and-two-stack`), WIN-004 (multifamily full-stack analog), Big Three Plays diagnostic (as the upside).
- **Permission:** Industry-only. Describe as "a 450,000 RSF Class A multi-tenant office property" or "a 20-year-old Class A office asset." Do not name the customer or location.

## WIN-006 — Voltage Spike Caught by Power Monitoring (~300K SF Mixed-Use) | Industry-only

A 300,000 SF mixed-use property — half office, half apartments — had large rooftop units on the office side that were being hammered with voltage spikes, and voltage drops were way out of spec. None of it was visible to the property team because nobody was watching power-quality data; the rooftop units were quietly being beat to death every day. OpticWise had alarms on the voltage monitoring as part of routine power-quality oversight on the owner-controlled network. When the alarms tripped, the team pulled the data, gave it to the owner, and the owner filed a utility claim based on the documented evidence. The utility had to come out and redo the feeder circuit and the transformer at the utility's expense. The fix probably saved the owner approximately a quarter million dollars by avoiding premature replacement of those rooftop units three or four years earlier than scheduled.

- **Why it matters:** Proves the difference between a network that delivers Wi-Fi and a network that protects capital. Power-quality monitoring is a Layer 1 instrument that lives on the owner-controlled backbone — it isn't a separate product, a separate vendor, or a separate CapEx line. It runs because the data & digital infrastructure is owner-controlled and the routine standards include alarming on out-of-spec voltage. Without that standard, the spikes would have kept hammering the rooftop units until they failed, and the owner would have paid for the replacement themselves with no recourse against the utility because there would have been no documented evidence.
- **Owner cost avoided:** Approximately $250,000 in premature rooftop-unit replacement CapEx, plus three to four years of additional useful life preserved on the existing equipment. Utility paid for the feeder circuit and transformer fix.
- **OpticWise takeaway:** Power quality is data. Data is an asset. An owner-controlled network with the right standards captures that data routinely and converts it into insurance claims, utility claims, warranty claims, and predictive maintenance — none of which a fragmented vendor stack can do because nobody on a fragmented stack owns the data.
- **PPP 5C:** Connect, Collect, Coordinate, Control
- **Use when:** Owner asks about predictive maintenance; owner asks "what kind of data actually matters at the property level"; owner is skeptical that operational data has dollar value; owner has aging mechanical equipment and is planning CapEx replacement cycles; AM is exploring whether digital infrastructure protects capital or just delivers connectivity; mixed-use or office property with rooftop HVAC.
- **Pairs with:** WIN-001 (Shelfware to Savings — different property, same principle: data already exists, just needs to be operationalized); NM-001 (Rogue Network Time Bomb — same root cause inverted: governance debt vs. governance discipline); Big Three Plays (Utilities); Two-Stack NOI Model (this is the expense-stack / Utilities Play in action, manifested as event-driven CapEx protection).
- **Permission:** Industry-only. Describe as "a 300,000 SF mixed-use property (half office, half multifamily)." Do not name the customer or location.
