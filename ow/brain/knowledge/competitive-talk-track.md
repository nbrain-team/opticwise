## Column A — OpticWise (what we deliver)

- **Owner-controlled intelligence layer (Property Brain™ → Portfolio Brain™).** Layer 2 — the product. A vendor- and LLM-agnostic governed data plane + trust plane that makes each property capable of autonomous activities and intelligence. Standardize once; intelligence compounds across the portfolio instead of restarting at every address. This is what competitors cannot build without years of consultative work, owner trust, and category IP.

- **Managed data & digital infrastructure (Layer 1) — the owned foundation.** Layer 1 delivers standalone value; an owner can start here and add Layer 2 when ready. Layer 1 is delivered as the **SIC®** platform — both a design strategy and a certified platform: the hardened, secure, infrastructure-connected platform that Layer 1 services are designed to be. SIC® is the result, the deliverable — not merely a standard or framework.

- **Components within the SIC® platform:**
  - **BoT® (Building of Things®)** — the standard. The owner-controlled backbone encompassing all sensors and connected "things." OW's term used instead of "IoT." Consolidates and governs building connectivity so every device or system runs on a single, secure, segmented foundation.
  - **CaaS (Connectivity-as-a-Service)** — the connectivity component within the SIC® platform. One piece of the managed-infrastructure foundation — never the headline, never positioning OW as a connectivity/Wi-Fi provider. **ElasticISP®** is an optional piece within CaaS: owner-controlled, ISP-agnostic managed connectivity with dual-circuit redundancy at the MPOE.
  - **5S® UX** — the user-experience standard across Layer 1: Seamless Mobility · Security · Stability (resilience) · Speed · Service. Privacy is one of the five S's. 5S® is a UX, not a platform.

- **Vendor-agnostic by design.** Plug in any decision engine, swap any vendor or model over time without losing data, governance, or portfolio intelligence. Portability changes the power dynamic — vendors compete to serve, not to trap. Export rights, admin credentials, and portability are written into contracts.

- **The core differentiation:** Competitors sell connectivity (or a dashboard) AS the product. OpticWise delivers connectivity as one component of an owned foundation beneath an owner-controlled intelligence layer. Most competitors operate in a single tier. OpticWise operates the two tiers that own and govern everything else.

## Column B — Best Available Alternative (what they deliver)

- **Fragmented + vendor-centric.** The owner pieces together energy, access, sensors, and apps from separate vendors; networks are a black box run by someone else. No single owner-controlled data plane. No governance across vendors.

- **Platforms without the foundation.** Tenant experience platforms and analytics vendors build at the UX or dashboard layer — dependent on whoever owns the data plane underneath. They are riders, not the chassis. Without an owner-controlled foundation, their products live as bolt-ons.

- **Data gravity.** The owner's data lives in vendor SaaS. Exports are limited, portability is absent, and the owner legally owns data they can't practically access or use. ("You own the car, but it's 3,000 miles away.")

- **Feature metrics, not outcomes.** Savings and experience gains are reported in silos and framed as vendor features — hard to convert to NOI. The owner gets dashboards, not drivers.

## Outcomes (locked canon — never a pledge, never a point estimate)

OpticWise does not make outcome pledges. The locked NOI benchmarks are ranges representing the realistic compounded effect of the Big Three Plays (utilities, insurance, occupancy) under owner control:

- **Multifamily:** $500–$600 per door per year (expense stack)
- **Multi-tenant office:** $0.60–$0.90 per rentable square foot per year (expense stack)

The income stack (5S® WiFi-as-amenity, ancillary tenant-facing services) is separate and additive. The blended NOI — income stack + expense stack — is what the owner books. Always quote as ranges. Always anchor at portfolio scale.

See `noi-benchmarks-and-two-stack` for full benchmark rules, the Two-Stack NOI Model, and proof-point mapping (WIN-004, WIN-005).

## Entry point

The canonical first engagement is the **PPP Review** — mapped, scoped, paid. It surfaces ownership, leakage, and the practical path to control. From there: one-building pilot (prove portability and governance), then scale.
