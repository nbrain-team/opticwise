## The opener that started the book

"Why in the world do you let a company put a network in your building that you don't own and that they mine data from your customers?"

Follow-up the moment they nod or say "we don't":

"But you do it every day with your ISP bulk agreements. Their network isn't actually yours."

## The reframe that resets the meeting

"This isn't an internet conversation. It's an owner-control digital conversation."

## The diligence wedge

"Make this review part of your diligence process. You won't know what you don't know."

## The exit-horizon objection killer

"Anytime we get somebody that starts monetizing $40–50 NOI per door, it goes right to the bottom line when they sell it. So the quicker you do it, the more NOI you can show."

## The ISP empathy probe

"Have you ever gotten good service from an ISP, or have you ever gotten private internet from an ISP?"

## The ROI principle

"Technology should have an ROI. It's another asset that should generate far more income than it costs and have a known return. If it's not, it should be restructured."

## Right Butt, Wrong Seat (Jim Collins / Good to Great)

The structural mismatch when PM/IT/AM are asked to run OT.

**Baseball variant (canonical default):**

"Jim Collins, Good to Great — right person, right seat. CRE is doing the opposite: right people, wrong seat. We're asking PMs, IT managers, and asset managers to run operational technology. They're capable. They're not skilled or trained for it. It's like asking your pitcher to be your pinch hitter. Could he stand there and swing? Sure. He's batting .130. Your pinch hitter's at .425. Different position. Different practice."

**Football variant (BNN, April 2026):**

"You wouldn't ask your running back to play middle linebacker. They can stand in the position, but they're not going to perform the role at the level you need. The skill sets are different. The training is different. The outcomes reflect that."

**Always pair with the resolution:**

"Don't ask them to staff up. Educate, partner, add a digital specialist."

**When to use which:** Default to baseball. Switch to football for football-fluent owners, or when you've already used the baseball variant in the same conversation.

See also `analogy-library` for full usage guidance (best contexts for each variant: owner-facing discovery, board decks, sales call openers).

## The Skybox Principle (portfolio-scale reframe)

"Don't manage a 50-asset portfolio from the field. Build the owner-controlled intelligence layer so you can operate from the skybox — seeing causes, not just results."

Use when the conversation moves from a single building to portfolio strategy, or when an asset manager describes managing through reports they can't see behind.
