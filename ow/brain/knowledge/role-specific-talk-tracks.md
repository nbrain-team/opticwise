## Asset Manager

### AM openers and reframes

**The Salwasser opener.** Use when the prospect is unclear on whether they're talking to a PM problem or an AM problem.
> "Property management keeps the building running. Asset management runs the investment. The data & digital infrastructure decision lives in the asset-management seat — because it shows up in valuation, debt, CapEx returns, and exit. We meet you in that seat."

**The cap-rate leverage line.** Use early when the AM is hearing OpEx-savings numbers and you want them capitalized.
> "Every dollar of recoverable NOI is worth roughly fifteen to twenty-five dollars of asset value at typical cap rates. The math doesn't care whether the dollar comes from a rent bump or an expense line. It only cares that you can defend the dollar in diligence."

**The refi-cliff frame.** Use with owners facing 2026–2027 maturities.
> "The refi package the lender wants is documented expense discipline and an asset-quality narrative — not the rate environment. The owners clearing the bar are the ones who own the operating data behind their NOI. The rest are negotiating from the lender's side of the table."

**The investor-letter test.** Use to puncture vendor-driven status quo.
> "If you had to write next quarter's investor letter today, what's the operating story you'd tell? Now: how much of that story sits in vendor platforms you can't pull from?"

**The portfolio-standard frame.** Use when the conversation is one asset and the prospect operates many.
> "One asset is a pilot. The point is the standard. Solve it once, encode the rules, run the playbook across the portfolio. That's where compounding shows up — not on the first building."

**The diligence-checklist frame.** Use for institutional capital and sponsors with regular trade activity.
> "Make this part of your diligence checklist on the next acquisition. Then make it part of your asset-management standard before the next disposition. You'll see the same gap from both sides — and once you've seen it, you can't unsee it."

### AM-targeted CTAs

- **Primary:** *Start with a PPP Review on one asset. Map ownership, leakage, and the path to control. Capitalize the upside before exit.*
- **First-call alternative:** *Run the Big Three diagnostic on one asset. Find the accountability-to-visibility gap. Scope a pilot around that play.*
- **Pre-refi specific:** *Run a 90-day pre-refi expense narrative. Bring documented operating control to the lender's table, not the rate environment.*
- **Portfolio-strategy specific:** *Encode the operating standard once. Scale across the portfolio. Compound the NOI uplift into the next valuation cycle.*
- **Diligence specific:** *Make the OW review part of your next acquisition diligence checklist — and your next disposition standard.*

### AM-first first-call structure (30 minutes)

Replace generic discovery flow with this structure when the seat is asset management:

1. **Open with the Big Three framing question.** "Three plays drive most of the NOI lift in CRE optimization — utilities, insurance, occupancy. Which of the three are you most accountable for, and where do you have the least operating visibility right now?"

2. **Capital posture.** Hold horizon. Refi calendar. Recent diligence experience (theirs as buyer or seller). Investor-reporting cadence. Where they feel most exposed in the next 12–24 months.

3. **Outcomes & accountability.** NOI variance to budget. Insurance renewal trajectory. Utility cost trajectory. Retention. Which line items they have actual control of versus the ones they hope move the right direction.

4. **The visibility gap.** What operating data do they receive vs. what they need? Where do reports leave the context out (NM-005 Massaged Report)? Have they run a play in the last twelve months that they couldn't fully measure?

5. **Control posture.** Who owns the data. How many networks. Contract export rights. PM model and whether their tools are non-negotiable. The MPOE and ISP picture.

6. **The one-property path.** PPP Review / Decision Stack Session on one asset. Map ownership, leakage, and a practical path to control. Pilot the standard. Scale the standard.

7. **Close with decision.** Schedule the review or disqualify fast.

**AM-targeted close lines (pick one):**

> *"The path forward is one building — map it, fix it, pilot the standard. If the math doesn't capitalize cleanly at exit, you keep the audit and walk away."*

> *"Bring this into your next diligence checklist. You won't know what you don't know until you do."*

> *"The data to run the Big Three is already being generated in your buildings. The question is whether you own it or your vendors do — and whether the next diligence team prices the gap before you do."*

## Owner

### Owner openers and reframes

**The control-nerve opener.** Use to reframe from "internet/tech" to "ownership."
> "You own the building. Do you own what it knows? Right now, the intelligence your building generates — who uses what, when, how — lives in vendor platforms. You own the asset; they own the asset's data. That's the conversation."

**The cost-of-waiting frame.** Use to convert status-quo bias into urgency without pressure tactics.
> "Status quo feels free because the bill is hidden. It shows up at the refinance and at the sale, when the other side's diligence finds the NOI you didn't capture — and prices it against you. The question isn't whether you pay. It's whether you capture it first."

**The legacy / long-game frame.** Use with principals who think generationally.
> "You didn't build this to win a quarter. The owners who win the next decade own the digital layer of their assets — because that's where the financing edge, the AI upside, and the exit premium are going to live. Owning it now is how you build for the long game."

**The decision-forcing reframe.** Use the moment the owner deflects or stalls.
> "I don't sell technology. I help owners decide whether they want their asset to perform. So which are you optimizing for — keeping the cost line flat, or making the asset perform? Because those are two different decisions, and only one of them is yours to make."

**The reversible-first-step close.** Use to dissolve overload into a single small yes.
> "You don't have to decide on the portfolio. Decide on one building. We map it, you see exactly what you own and what you're leaving on the table, and then you decide whether to kill it or scale it. One building. Reversible. Facts, not a pitch."

### Owner-targeted CTAs

- **Primary:** *Start with a complimentary PPP Review on one building. Get the Property Data Map, the Control Gap Analysis, and a prioritized Roadmap. See exactly what you own — and what you don't.*
- **Cost-of-waiting:** *Run the review before your next refinance or disposition. Capitalize the recoverable NOI yourself, before the other side's diligence prices it against you.*
- **Control-led:** *Map who owns your data & digital infrastructure today. If the answer is "the vendors," let's change it on one building and prove the model.*
- **Legacy-led:** *Make owner-controlled data & digital infrastructure the standard for the asset you're building to last. Crawl, walk, run — one building first.*
- **Decision-forcing:** *Decide one thing: are you optimizing for cost, or for asset performance? Then let's act on the answer.*

### Owner-first first-call structure (30 minutes)

Use this structure when the principal is the decision-maker.

1. **Open on control, not technology.** The book opener: *"Why would you let a company put a network in your building that you don't own?"* — then the bulk-agreement turn. Reset the meeting: *"This isn't an internet conversation. It's an owner-control conversation."*

2. **Surface where control has already slipped.** Who owns the network? How many vendor systems? What's in the contracts — export rights, admin credentials, portability? Most owners discover, in this conversation, that they don't know — and that the not-knowing is the point.

3. **Name the cost of waiting.** Refi calendar. Hold horizon. Recent or upcoming diligence (as buyer or seller). The diligence discount (NM-006). Make status-quo bias visible and expensive.

4. **Translate to capitalized value — in their terms.** Recoverable NOI × cap rate as *your equity at exit.* Anchor with the canonical benchmarks as ranges and the WIN-004/WIN-005 proof. Then apply the ROI principle: an asset with a known return, not a cost line.

5. **Elevate to legacy.** What are they building? For how long? Tie owner-controlled data & digital infrastructure to the durable, financeable, AI-ready, hand-it-down quality of the asset.

6. **Shrink the decision.** Crawl / walk / run. One building. The complimentary PPP Audit — Property Data Map, Control Gap Analysis, Roadmap. Reversible. No commitment past the review.

7. **Force the decision.** Don't accept "I'll ask my team." Align the owner on the decision first; bring the team in to implement, not decide. Schedule the review or disqualify fast.

**Owner-targeted close lines (pick one):**

> *"One building. We map what you own, what you're leaving on the table, and the path to control. If the math doesn't work, you keep the map and walk away."*

> *"If you can't answer who owns the network, the review writes itself. Want to map it in one building and decide with facts?"*

> *"You own the building. Let's make sure you own what it knows — before the next refinance or the next buyer's diligence team does the math for you."*

## IT Manager

### IT openers and reframes

**The OT-strategy gap.** Use to open, and to reframe the conversation away from "another vendor."
> "You almost certainly have an IT strategy — email, identity, the PM platform, all governed. Does the owner have an OT strategy? The cameras, the access control, the HVAC controllers, the dozen vendor networks running the building? That's the plane vendors quietly control, and it's the one your cyber renewal is starting to ask about."

**The attack-surface line.** Use the moment security gets raised — turn the objection into the reason.
> "Every vendor that put a network in this building added a door you're accountable for but can't see. We consolidate them under one segmented, documented backplane — and we operate it. Your surface gets smaller, not bigger, and it's not another thing on your plate."

**The not-your-seat line.** Use early and explicitly to defuse the turf reflex.
> "We're not coming for your domain. Corporate IT — email, ERP, the PM system — stays yours. We take the OT plane: the seat nobody was ever staffed to sit in. You get to focus on the job you were actually hired for."

**The cyber-renewal line.** Use with any owner facing an insurance renewal or audit.
> "When the renewal questionnaire asks how the building systems are segmented and who holds the credentials, what's today's honest answer? We make the answer 'here's the map, here's the segmentation, here's the export' — instead of a shrug and a vendor you can't reach."

**The credential-ownership line.** Use to make "owner-controlled" concrete for a skeptic.
> "Owner-controlled isn't a slogan. It means the admin credentials live with the owner, the data is exportable, and portability is in the contract. You can revoke any vendor, replace any system, and pull your data — without rewiring the building. That's a security posture, not a sales pitch."

### IT-targeted CTAs

- **Primary:** *Run a PPP Review on one building — let us map the OT plane, every vendor network, every connected system. You'll see your real attack surface, documented, in one place.*
- **Security-led alternative:** *Before the next cyber renewal, let us build the segmentation-and-inventory map for the building systems. Walk into the renewal with documentation instead of a shrug.*
- **Scope-boundary close:** *Corporate IT stays yours. Let us own the OT plane and operate it. You add zero headcount and lose your worst exposure.*
- **Champion-enabling:** *Give us the technical questions and the security objections. We'll answer all of them in writing so you can take a clean recommendation to the owner.*

## Property Manager

### PM openers and reframes

**The bandwidth-relief opener.** Lead with this in almost every PM conversation.
> "You're already running this building with the team you have. This doesn't add to that — it takes off it. Every connectivity support call, 24/7, comes to us, not you. You stop fielding tickets for a service you don't bill or own."

**The new-thermostat line.** Use to frame connectivity as the PM's retention lever.
> "Connectivity is the new thermostat — invisible when it works, a one-star review when it doesn't. We make it work and we support it. The win shows up in your retention and your reviews — and that's your story to tell."

**The cadence-control line.** Use the moment "disruption" or "transitioning residents" comes up.
> "No rip-and-replace. You pick the cadence — capture new leases first, phase the rest around turnover. You stay in control of the day-to-day. We just handle the plane underneath it."

**The one-number line.** Use when the PM describes vendor chaos or finger-pointing.
> "Today an outage is a scavenger hunt — which of six vendors do you call, and which one blames the other? One operator. One number. One accountable party. Fewer fires, and none of them yours to fight alone."

**The look-good-upstairs line.** Use to give the PM a reason to advocate internally.
> "When the asset manager asks why retention's up and tickets are down, that's your story. We do the work in the background; you get the numbers in the foreground."

### PM-targeted CTAs

- **Primary:** *Run a PPP Review on one building. We map what's there, what's breaking, and what we take off your plate — before anything changes in your day-to-day.*
- **Relief-led:** *Let us take the connectivity support calls for one building, on your cadence. You'll feel the difference in the first month.*
- **Retention-led:** *Make seamless, supported connectivity your leasing and renewal story. We build and run it; you lease against it.*
- **Champion-enabling:** *Tell us your worst recurring tenant-tech headache. If we can make it disappear on one building, you'll have the story to take to the owner.*
