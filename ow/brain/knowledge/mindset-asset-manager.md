Default audience lens for all owner-facing outputs unless a specific brief calls for a different audience.

## Why the AM matters

Asset managers control the capital allocation conversation. They run the investment — valuation, debt, CapEx returns, refinancing, portfolio strategy. When OpticWise wins, it wins because an AM said yes.

## The Salwasser distinction

Property management keeps a building running. Asset management runs the investment. The AM's decisions drive long-term results. This is the seat OpticWise needs to be across from.

## AM psychology

- **Financial quants, not operators.** Spreadsheets, board decks, investor letters.
- **Accountable for outcomes they can't fully influence.** Inherit acquisition-team decisions.
- **Live in lagging summaries, not leading drivers.** NM-005 (Massaged Report) pattern.
- **Respect ROI math; reject anything that can't show capitalized value.**
- **Hate surprises.** Insurance shocks, cap-rate moves, diligence findings.
- **Skeptical of "PropTech."** A hundred dashboard demos have dulled the blade.

## 2026 AM context

- **Refi pressure:** ~17% of office mortgage balances mature in 2026. Loans at 3–4% repricing to 7%+.
- **Insurance:** Property insurance up 30–50% most markets, 60–100% coastal.
- **Capital is selective, not scarce.** Operational value creation is the investable edge.

## Six AM activities mapped to OpticWise

1. **Valuation** → Big Three Plays. NOI × cap rate. Every dollar of recoverable NOI = $14–$25 of asset value.
2. **Property tax appeals** → Owner-controlled operating data supports appeal narrative.
3. **Debt / mortgage review** → Expense control = DSCR resilience.
4. **Capital improvements** → Digital CapEx is real CapEx with documented ROI.
5. **Refinancing** → Renewal-ready data package wins better terms.
6. **Portfolio strategy** → Property Brain™ → Portfolio Brain™. The Skybox Principle.

## AM language patterns

Use: NOI × cap rate, DSCR, LTV, IRR, hold-period economics, refi math, diligence findings, expense trajectory, operational value creation, capitalized savings.

| PM-coded (avoid with AM) | AM-coded (use) |
|---|---|
| Tickets, work orders | Operating risk, OpEx variance |
| Tenant complaints | Retention exposure, lease-up velocity |
| Outages | Brand-reputation events, insurance-claim exposure |
| Dashboard | Capitalized visibility |
| IT spend | Digital CapEx with documented ROI |
| Vendor sprawl | Counterparty risk |
| Smart building | Owner-controlled operating intelligence |

## The villain (AM frame)

> "Vendor-controlled data is the silent NOI tax that doesn't show up on your P&L until diligence finds it. By then, the price has already moved."

## What NOT to do

- Do not lead with PM-coded pain (tickets, helpdesk volume)
- Do not lead with feature lists (SIC®, BoT® are differentiators, not openers)
- Do not pitch "smart building" or "PropTech"
- Do not present uncapitalized savings — always capitalize at cap rate
- Do not assume the AM has time to learn — pre-translate everything
