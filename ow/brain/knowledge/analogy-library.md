Use these in writing and speaking when an owner needs to *feel* the gap, not just hear about it. All three analogies are source-approved for public use (sales calls, blogs, LinkedIn, talk tracks).

## The 3,000-Mile Car

**Translates:** Legal data ownership vs. practical data access.

> "Legally, you might own your data — but you don't have it. It's like owning a car on the other side of the country. You could say, 'I could drive to the store.' No, you can't. You do own the car. But it's 3,000 miles away. You can't use it."

- **Why this lands:** It collapses the entire vendor-lock-in problem into one mental image any owner gets in two seconds.
- **Best for:** First-call discovery, LinkedIn content, blog hooks.
- **Pairs with:** "If you don't own your data & digital infrastructure, your vendors do."

## The Hand Specialist

**Translates:** Why operational technology is its own discipline, not just IT.

> "I had a wrist issue. I went to a hand specialist — not my general doctor. She walked in, said 'I know what that is,' did some manipulation, and three days later it was fine. Because she sees ten hands a day. That's what you need. The return on a digital investment is massive — but you need the specialist."

- **Why this lands:** Defuses the "we already have an IT person" objection without insulting anyone's IT person. Makes the case that OT is its own discipline.
- **Best for:** Sales calls, content, IT-manager conversations.

## Right Butt, Wrong Seat (Jim Collins / Good to Great)

**Translates:** The structural mismatch when PM/IT/AM are asked to run OT.

**Baseball variant (canonical default):**

> "Jim Collins, Good to Great — right person, right seat. CRE is doing the opposite: right people, wrong seat. We're asking PMs, IT managers, and asset managers to run operational technology. They're capable. They're not skilled or trained for it. It's like asking your pitcher to be your pinch hitter. Could he stand there and swing? Sure. He's batting .130. Your pinch hitter's at .425. Different position. Different practice."

**Football variant:**

> "You wouldn't ask your running back to play middle linebacker. They can stand in the position, but they're not going to perform the role at the level you need. The skill sets are different. The training is different. The outcomes reflect that."

- **Best for:** Owner-facing discovery, board decks, sales call openers. Use football variant for football-fluent owners or when baseball variant was already used.
- **Always pair with the resolution:** "Don't ask them to staff up. Educate, partner, add a digital specialist."
