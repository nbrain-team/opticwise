**Permanently internal — references a difficult-PM archetype for internal sales coaching; never client-facing, never shareable, not a launch-pass candidate.**

Source: Friction PM Archetype Playbook + Sales Playbook section 16.

## The mental model (so you don't take the bait)

This person is Compliance + Risk + Budget Protection, not the buyer.
- Uses ambiguity as leverage ("where is this written?").
- Defaults to extended calls and circular arguments.
- Turns admin friction into renegotiation (invoice format → economic model debate).

**Your job:** control the interface, protect decision rights, and convert narratives into decisions.

## Non-negotiable operating principles

### 1. Split every issue into two tracks

- **Track A — Accuracy / Ops (PM lane):** duplicates, dates, SF, line items, invoice formatting, missing backup.
- **Track B — Commercial Model (Owner lane):** pricing rules/minimums, CapEx ownership, rev share, contract terms.

### 2. Never debate the paragraph

Convert long narratives into a checklist or worksheet. If it's not itemized, it's not actionable.

### 3. No model changes via invoice thread

Invoice threads can resolve accuracy only. Model changes require a scheduled owner call.

### 4. Timebox everything

Calls: 15 minutes. Email replies: ≤ 8 sentences. If it can't be closed fast, it gets escalated to the owner with a decision agenda.

### 5. Document the "Rules of the Road"

Create a one-page artifact for the property that clearly states: what the building pays (CapEx / OpEx buckets), what tenants pay (only what they signed), pricing rule + any minimums, rev share rule (if any) + where it shows up, and simple payback logic (CapEx → MRC → profit after payback).

## Email rules that stop spirals

### The 4-move response pattern

1. Acknowledge (one line)
2. Separate tracks (A vs B)
3. Request specifics in required format
4. Next step + deadline

### Required format (copy/paste)

> Please send the specific items in this format so we can reconcile quickly:
> **Invoice # | Line item | Amount | Issue (error/question) | Requested action**

If they send paragraphs again:

> "Thanks — to resolve quickly, please re-send as the line-item list above. Once we have that, we'll reconcile and credit anything incorrect."

### Default "Track A / Track B" email template

> Understood.
> **Track A (invoice accuracy):** please send the specific line items you believe are incorrect (Invoice #, line item, amount, reason). We'll reconcile and credit anything wrong.
> **Track B (commercial model):** pricing minimums / rev share / CapEx vs tenant charges are owner-level decisions. We'll confirm with the owner and document a one-page Rules of the Road so this doesn't get re-litigated monthly.
> Once I have the line-item list, we'll close Track A immediately.

### "Stop the thread / reset process" template

> I'm going to pause this thread so we don't keep re-working the same assumptions.
> Next step: send the line-item list (Invoice # | line item | amount | issue | requested action). We'll reconcile within X business days.
> Any changes to pricing / rev share / CapEx rules will be handled in an owner alignment call.

### "Owner escalation" template (calm, directive)

> This appears to be a commercial model question (not an invoice accuracy issue).
> Let's schedule 20 minutes with the owner to confirm the model and document it in writing. Once confirmed, you'll have a one-pager to reference going forward.

## In-person: short, confident, non-defensive

Use this one-liner when they posture:

> "Totally fair to reconcile accuracy. The business model is an owner decision — our job is to operate inside it."

## Call control: how to stop long loops

### 20-second opener (sets the frame)

> "I've got 15 minutes. Goal is to close two items: (1) invoice accuracy list, (2) what requires an owner decision. If we drift, I'll pull us back."

### Polite interrupt that works

> "Let me pause you for a second so we don't lose the thread."

### The 3 spine questions (use to steer)

- "Which invoice number are we talking about?"
- "Is this accuracy or commercial model?"
- "What is the specific outcome you want from this call?"

### Close with a recap (always)

> "Great — I'm sending a recap with (a) what we're correcting/crediting and (b) what's an owner decision. That's the process going forward."

If they keep looping:

> "We're not going to re-debate the model on this call. If you want to revisit the model, we'll do that with the owner."

## Do / Don't

**DO:** Respond to facts, not tone. Convert narrative into line items. Keep replies short and directive. Separate accuracy from model every time. Escalate model questions to the owner with a tight agenda. Create "Rules of the Road" and reference it constantly.

**DON'T:** Get pulled into "explain it again" loops. Debate rev share/pricing in an invoice email chain. Accept vague claims ("duplicates somewhere") without the list. Let calls run open-ended. Try to "win" — your goal is closure + governance.

## Quick Slack snippet (ultra-short version)

**Split Track A vs Track B.** Track A = accuracy/duplicates → send list (invoice#, line item, $, reason). Track B = pricing/rev share/CapEx → owner decision, scheduled call, document "Rules of the Road." No paragraphs, no long calls, no model debates in invoice threads. Timebox, recap, enforce process.

## Success criteria

- Invoice disputes close in ≤ 5 business days once the line-item list is received.
- Email threads stay ≤ 6 messages total.
- Commercial model questions happen only in owner alignment calls.
- Fewer "re-litigations" month over month.
