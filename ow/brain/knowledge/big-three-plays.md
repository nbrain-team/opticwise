A first-call sales tool. Three plays — utilities, insurance, occupancy — produce most of the NOI uplift in CRE optimization. They are **not** the only plays — they are **the big three**.

**Why these three:** The PPP book (Chapter 5) names them as "the three areas that typically have the biggest impact on your bottom line." Asset managers are accountable for outcomes in all three, but they generally make decisions in all three with incomplete operating data — because the data lives in vendor platforms they can't reach.

## Play 1 — Utilities (the offensive line)

**The play:** Demand management + consumption visibility + tenant-allocation accuracy.

**What's invisible without owner-accessible data:** HVAC peak-demand timing, motor-start staggering, tenant-by-tenant consumption, dormant lighting and automation systems.

**Quick plays:** HVAC demand-curve delay (a 7-minute stagger to avoid the peak surge); sub-metering by actual tenant use (see WIN-003); activate the lighting control system that's already installed (see WIN-001).

**Canonical proof point:** WIN-006 (Voltage Spike Caught by Power Monitoring) is a canonical proof point for the Utilities lever — power-quality monitoring → utility claim → CapEx protection.

**Pairs with:** WIN-001, WIN-003, WIN-006, NM-005.

## Play 2 — Insurance (the defensive line)

**The play:** Premium reduction at renewal + deductible reduction + better claims documentation.

**What's invisible without owner-accessible data:** Leak detection logs, alarm response times, access control logs, SOPs around water/fire/security events.

**Quick plays:** Build a renewal-ready data package (the underwriter responds to documentation); demonstrate response protocol with system logs to argue for a meaningful premium reduction; surface the claims-history narrative *before* the underwriter does; deploy proactive leak detection so you're managing leaks before tenants call them in (and the response is documented for renewal).

**Pairs with:** NM-005 — most owners walk into renewal with no data package because the data sits in vendor silos.

## Play 3 — Occupancy (the special teams)

**The play:** Space utilization revenue + dynamic pricing + tenant retention + lease-up velocity.

**What's invisible without owner-accessible data:** Amenity usage patterns, parking demand by time-of-day, gym/lounge utilization, foot-traffic patterns, pre-move-out signal patterns.

**Quick plays:** Dynamic parking pricing tied to local event schedules; reposition under-used common areas based on actual utilization; surface pre-move-out signals to operations *before* tenants give notice.

**Pairs with:** WIN-002, NM-006.

## How to use this diagnostic on a first call

1. Open with: *"Three plays drive most of the NOI lift in CRE optimization — utilities, insurance, occupancy. Which of the three are you most accountable for, and where do you have the least operating visibility right now?"*

2. Listen for the gap between accountability and visibility. That gap is where the conversation goes.

3. Map their answer to the relevant nightmares and wins from this library.

4. Frame the close: *"The data to play these is already being generated in your buildings. The question is whether you own it or your vendors do."*

## Important framing notes

- These are **the big three** — not the only plays. Other plays exist on the expense stack (greenfield CapEx avoidance, ESG/compliance positioning, etc.). Income-stack plays (5S® WiFi-as-amenity, ancillary tenant-facing services) form the parallel stack — see `noi-benchmarks-and-two-stack`. The Big Three are the highest-leverage, fastest-to-act plays on the expense side for most owners.

- Do not present this as "the OpticWise framework." It's a diagnostic tool. The underlying frameworks remain PPP 5C™ and the Two-Layer Model.

- PPP 5C mapping for the diagnostic itself: Clarify (to find the gaps) → Collect (to feed the plays) → Control (to act on them).

- Always pair with the canonical NOI benchmarks. The Big Three are how you produce the $500–$600/door or $0.60–$0.90/RSF uplift — not via any one of them alone.
