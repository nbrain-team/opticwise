Owner Play: Stop Letting Vendors Own Your Data & Digital Infrastructure

A simple monthly play to reclaim control without adding burden to your on-site team.

If you don’t own your data & digital infrastructure, your vendors do.

Most commercial real estate owners didn’t lose control of their buildings on purpose. It happened one contract at a time, one “smart” system at a time, one vendor dashboard at a time.

Each decision felt tactical. Together, they created a structural disadvantage: your building’s intelligence became someone else’s asset.

The Three Questions That Reveal Who Controls Your Building

Before you evaluate any technology, run any audit, or sign any contract, ask these three questions about every system in your building:

  1. Who holds admin credentials? If the answer is your vendor, you don’t control the system—they do.
  2. Can you export clean data in a standard format? If data is only accessible through a vendor dashboard, it’s not your data. It’s their leverage.
  3. Can you swap the platform without rewiring the building? If changing vendors means ripping out infrastructure, you’ve designed lock-in, not flexibility.

If you answered “no” or “I don’t know” to any of these, your vendor has more control over your building’s economics than you do.

The Monthly Play: Admin Credentials Review

This is a recurring play—not a one-time project. Run it monthly or quarterly, and it compounds.

Step 1: Inventory

List every system, platform, and network in the building. For each one, document who holds admin credentials today.

Step 2: Reclaim

For every system where a vendor holds admin credentials, request transfer to owner-controlled accounts. If they refuse, you’ve found a control gap.

Step 3: Document

Create an ownership map: what you control, what you don’t, and where value is leaking. This becomes the foundation for standardization across the portfolio.

Step 4: Govern

Set rules for new systems: no vendor gets admin access without documented governance. No shadow networks. No data that can’t be exported.

The compounding effect: Run this play across multiple buildings and you move from reactive, building-by-building management to portfolio-wide operational control. That’s the shift from Property Intelligence to Portfolio Intelligence.

Why This Matters for NOI

Every system where a vendor controls the data represents:

  • Revenue leakage through connectivity economics you don’t control
  • Operating cost through redundant infrastructure and manual workarounds
  • Risk exposure through undocumented access and ungoverned networks
  • Diligence weakness because you can’t prove what you don’t control

Ownership isn’t a dashboard. It’s a durable capability.
