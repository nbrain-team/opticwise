FAQ: Owning Your Data & Digital Infrastructure

Real questions from CRE professionals who are making the shift from vendor convenience to owner control. Find answers organized by your role—or explore the general hub below.

Layer 1: Managed Data & Digital Infrastructure
We’re designing a new building—how do we avoid designing vendor lock-in? +

Start with owner-controlled data & digital infrastructure standards before low-voltage decisions are locked. Segment networks, document governance, and ensure all admin credentials and data export rights stay with the owner—not the installing vendor. BoT® (Building of Things®) consolidates networks onto the fewest physical paths, reducing build cost and preventing the silo problem before it starts.

What should our low-voltage and network spec include to protect future flexibility? +

Repeatable standards for segmentation, access rules, and documentation. Every system should be designed for data export, governed identity, and platform portability. This is the foundation that makes future technology swaps possible without rewiring the building.

How does BoT® reduce build costs? +

By consolidating all your property’s networks onto the fewest possible physical networks. Instead of each vendor building their own path, BoT® creates a shared, governed infrastructure. This often saves hundreds of thousands in build costs and thousands per month in operating costs.

Layer 1: Managed Data & Digital Infrastructure
How do I know if my vendors own my data & digital infrastructure? +

Ask three questions: Who holds admin credentials? Can you export clean data in a standard format? Can you swap the platform without rewiring the building? If the answer to any of these is “no” or “I don’t know,” your vendor likely has more control than you do.

What does “managed data & digital infrastructure” actually include? +

Design, implementation, and ongoing operations of your building’s networks, wireless, and connected systems—all under owner-controlled standards. This includes network segmentation, access governance, documentation, performance monitoring, and coordination with vendors so your on-site team isn’t the digital help desk.

Will this replace our existing systems? +

Not necessarily. The goal is to create an owner-controlled foundation underneath your existing systems. You keep what works, consolidate what doesn’t, and gain the governance and portability that vendor-controlled setups deny you.
